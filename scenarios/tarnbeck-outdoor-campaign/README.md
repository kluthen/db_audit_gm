## 2. Background

Tarnbeck Outdoor, six months after the v0.3 audit (tarnbeck-outdoor-shop), now sells abroad, outside the customs union too. Its web shop writes through PgBouncer into the shop's PostgreSQL database, which refreshes its popular products every hour from its own crontab. Lumen Analytics, a BI agency, moved the back office's figures to a reporting database of its own: an hourly roll-up and a nightly close run from its transformer (bi), a churn table for a dashboard is refreshed by a pg_cron job inside the reporting database, and a nightly sales-detail roll-up runs from the reporting server's own crontab. The marketing team, in-house, runs the win-back campaign and the shipping notices, and the shipping manager the export declarations to the customs gateway, all from the crm transformer. Ines Varga, the operations manager, asks for an audit after complaints: unsubscribed customers still mailed, shipping notices sent twice, a promotion at 3 a.m. abroad, revenue that never matches the dashboard, dashboards frozen this morning, and a reporting database that "restarted itself" one night. She hands over the company's data policy with the story. Prometheus, an exporter and Grafana watch the shop's database only. The data files create the roles of access model 9.1 (shop, shop_app, shop_reports, shop_etl, shop_crm, shop_customs, customs_gw, shop_refresh on db; reporting, bi_etl, bi_reader on reporting); the brick creates dba, monitoring, gm_check and pgbouncer_lookup on each instance.

### `f10`: `purpose-heavy-job-on-database-node` on `reporting`, known

- **What is wrong**: Lumen's nightly sales-detail roll-up runs from reporting's own crontab at 03:00 as bi_etl: CALL rpt.sales_detail_rollup(), which builds every order changed in its window into one jsonb_agg in the database node's backend.
